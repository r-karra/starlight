# Future hardware

## Purpose
This document describes a long-term hardware vision for Starlight while keeping the focus on research and validation rather than immediate manufacturing.

## Background
Custom hardware can improve comfort, sensing quality, and device integration, but it should come after the interaction model is validated on existing devices. The goal is to define a research direction, not promise a near-term product. This document belongs primarily to the Vision layer, while remaining grounded in the lessons learned from the Today and Research phases.

## Goals
- Describe a reference hardware vision for glasses, phone, and laptop.
- Preserve the principle that custom hardware is a long-term goal.
- Keep the discussion grounded in interaction needs and platform constraints.

## System design
A future reference hardware stack could include:
- Glasses with a lightweight display, audio input and output, and comfortable form factor.
- Phone with stronger sensing and secure local processing for context and orchestration.
- Laptop with larger screen, richer input, and deeper document workflow support.

The design should be framed as a future reference architecture, not as a current shipping plan.

## Key workflows
- Capture and act on information with less friction than current consumer devices permit.
- Shift fluidly between glanceable assistance and full workstation workflows.
- Maintain privacy, battery life, and comfort across the devices.

## Constraints
- Hardware design must account for comfort, power, heat, and cost.
- Custom hardware should be justified by user studies, not by assumptions alone.
- The path should remain open to incremental evolution rather than a single disruptive leap.

## Future work
Future work should define reference requirements for display ergonomics, sensor placement, battery strategy, and maintenance of a clear upgrade path.
